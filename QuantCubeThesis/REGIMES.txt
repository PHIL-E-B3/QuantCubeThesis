
\subsection{Communication Regimes and Model Diagnostics}

  Rather than treating FOMC communication as a uniform stream of text, the LLM pipeline
  classifies each sentence along two axes: its linguistic character (descriptive versus
   interpretive) and the nature of any forward commitment (conditional versus
  unconditional). As well, the LLM flags each meeting for the presence of elevated uncertainty or
  asymmetric risk language. This allows the forecasting gains documented above to be
  traced back to specific features of central-bank communication. To do this, different masks can be applied as described below. All results shown apply for the best performing model LLM 4h.

  \paragraph{Descriptive versus interpretive sentences.} As discussed previously, descriptive sentences record
  current economic conditions as facts while interpretive sentences convey the committee's
  assessment, tone, or forward view. By only using interpretive sentences to construct the sentiment indicators, the model yields an 11.0\% RMSE improvement over the macro baseline (DM-$t = 1.23$, $p = 0.110$), compared with 14.1\% for the full document ($p = 0.008$). The
  interpretive component therefore carries the bulk of the forecasting signal.
  Descriptive sentences entered without interpretive context are actively harmful: the
  descriptive-plus-conditional specification produces a 14.1\% degradation in RMSE (DM-$t
   = -3.57$), indicating that stripped-of-tone factual language introduces noise rather
  than signal.

  \paragraph{Delphic versus Odyssean guidance.} Following
  \citet{campbelletal2012}, monetary policy sentences are classified as Delphic
   (conditional on incoming data) or Odyssean (unconditional commitments). As before, this can be explored by only including sentences that were labelled as expressing "unconditional commitment" (odyssean) or "none" (delphic). Interpretive
  sentences with Delphic character (Variant C) preserve an 8.8\% improvement (DM-$t =
  0.96$, $p = 0.168$), while Odyssean-only language (Variant D) reverses the gain to
  $-9.0\%$. Removing meetings that contain any unconditional commitment language entirely
   likewise worsens performance. The forecasting signal therefore lives in conditional,
  outlook-based language — the Fed communicating how it reads the data — not in firm
  pledges that, once made, are already priced.

  \paragraph{Risk and uncertainty regimes.} Each OOS meeting is classified by whether
  the meeting-window documents exhibit elevated uncertainty (high within-document
  sentiment width) or asymmetric risk language (upward or downward sentiment skew).
  During the 47 high-uncertainty meetings the sentiment model improves on the baseline
  by 17.4%, against 11.9% in the remaining 29 calmer periods. Upward-skew meetings show
  a 16.6% gain; downward-skew meetings show the largest improvement at 20.3%. Across all
