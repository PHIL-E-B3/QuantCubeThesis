- "economic_activity": GDP, output, consumption, investment, housing, retail, industrial production — SPECIFIC domestic output/demand variables only

Polarity by topic:
- inflation: rising/high = hawkish, falling/low = dovish
- labor_market: tight/low unemployment = hawkish, slack/high unemployment = dovish
- economic_activity: stronger growth = hawkish, weaker growth = dovish
- financial_conditions: COUNTERINTUITIVE — easier/accommodative = hawkish (Fed must do more), tighter/stressed = dovish
- monetary_policy: tightening, raising rates, stopping/slowing Fed purchases = hawkish, easing, lowering rates, starting/increasing Fed purchases = dovish

DEFAULT-TO-NEUTRAL:
- Pure data report with no comparative/directional reference → neutral. Need "above/below target", "stronger/weaker than expected", or directional language to go directional.
- Mention of FOMC, policy, rates, balance sheet → neutral unless explicit action signal present (raise, cut, tighten, ease, taper).
- Deliberation without action → neutral.
- Tie-break toward NEUTRAL. Signal must be IN the sentence.

Key rules:
- LEVEL OVERRIDES DIRECTION: "fell but remained high" → hawkish. "Fell but remained well above target" → hawkish.
- Negations: label STATE described. "Not seeing deanchoring" = dovish. "Has not returned to target" = hawkish. "Inflation subdued/moderate/at or below target" = dovish, not hawkish.
- Holds: "neutral" unless framed as restrictive (hawkish) or accommodative (dovish).
- Qualitative descriptors: "solid"/"robust" = hawkish when describing economic conditions.
- Minority views ("one member", "a few participants"): register the direction but do not let minority framing amplify beyond what the signal alone warrants.

"commitment": Non-monetary_policy → always false. For monetary_policy:
- true: specific action confirmed taken/authorized (past tense counts), or "will/intends to/is prepared to" + reference to policy action. Referenced conditions used to set specific objectives of policy or as justification for policy in which case, must be confirmed not possible/likely/hypothetical when used justification. Implicit negations count ("not the time to pause" = hiking commitment).
- false: modal softeners ("could", "would", "anticipates", "aim", "likely", "warranted"), unmet outcome conditions, participant preferences, generic mandate reaffirmations.


"risk": Populate from explicit risk mentions OR conditional/hypothetical structures ("if/should/were to") — resolve to the direction of the stated scenario.
- "skewed_downside": risks toward growth weakening, labor loosening/lower employment, financial stress, recession, or lower inflation.
- "skewed_upside": risks toward overheating, high growth or inflation overshoot. For inflation: skewed_upside = MORE inflation is the tail risk. Label captures direction, not whether outcome is good or bad.
- "symmetric": explicitly balanced ("roughly balanced", "risks on both sides", "two-sided risks") — requires affirmative balancing language; mere absence of skew ≠ symmetric.
- "na": no explicit risk framing and no conditional scenario present.


"width":
- "elevated": ANY explicit uncertainty acknowledgement. Triggers: "uncertain", "uncertainty", "unclear", "hard to gauge", "hard to say", "hard to know", "difficult to assess", "difficult to determine", "we don't know", "cannot know", "imperfectly understood", "unprecedented", "remains to be seen", "hard to predict". Bare "uncertain"/"uncertainty" qualifies. FIRES INDEPENDENTLY OF SENTIMENT.
- "none": baseline. Modal verbs, standard hedges, single-direction signals, opposing economic forces.
- risk and width can co-exist.
