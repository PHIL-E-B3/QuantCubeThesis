- Pure data report with no comparative/directional reference → neutral. Need "above/below target", "stronger/weaker than expected", or intensity marker to go directional.

- Deliberation without action → neutral.
- Tie-break toward NEUTRAL. Signal must be IN the sentence.

- LEVEL OVERRIDES DIRECTION: "fell but remained high" → hawkish. "Fell but remained well above target" → strongly_hawkish.
- Negations: label STATE described. "Not seeing deanchoring" = dovish. "Has not returned to target" = hawkish.
- Holds: "neutral" unless framed as restrictive (hawkish) or accommodative (dovish).

TEST: Can you point to BOTH a specific action AND explicit condition? If either missing → "none".

"risk":

- "symmetric": explicitly balanced ("roughly balanced", "risks on both sides") — do not resolve directionally.
- "na": no explicit risk framing. For conditionals, populate with direction of stated scenario.

"width":
- "elevated": ANY explicit uncertainty acknowledgement — "uncertain", "uncertainty", "unclear", "hard to gauge", "hard to say", "hard to know", "difficult to assess", "difficult to determine", "we don't know", "cannot know", "imperfectly understood", "unprecedented", "remains to be seen", "hard to predict", "wide range of outcomes", "difficult to quantify", "we can't be sure". Bare "uncertain"/"uncertainty" qualifies. FIRES INDEPENDENTLY OF SENTIMENT.
- "contested": two economic forces, same sentence, same tense, opposite policy directions. One hawkish in isolation, one dovish. Signals cancel → sentiment = neutral. Connectives: "but", "however", "while", "although", "even as", "despite", "yet".

  ✓ "Inflation above target while labor conditions softened." (dual-mandate collision)
  ✗ "Fell but remained high" — level governs.
  ✗ "Solid but uncertainty remains" — hedge ≠ force.
- "none": baseline.
- risk and width can co-exist.
